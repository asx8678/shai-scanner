## 🎯 Overview

## 🎨 Customization Process

### Step 1: Identify Stakeholder Type
Determine which stakeholder group you're targeting:
- **Technical**: Developers, DevOps, architects
- **Business**: Executives, managers, financial decision-makers
- **Security**: Security teams, compliance officers, risk managers
- **Community**: Open source contributors, users, advocates

### Step 2: Select Appropriate Content
From the template, select the stakeholder-specific version for each section:
- Subject lines
- Preview text
- Executive summary
- Key achievements
- Launch details
- Next steps
- Call-to-action
- Closing signature

### Step 3: Personalize Variables
Replace the following variables with actual values:
- `[Stakeholder Name]` - Recipient's full name
- `[Stakeholder Title]` - Recipient's job title
- `[Organization Name]` - Recipient's organization
- `[Launch Date]` - Actual launch date
- `[Primary Metric]` - Most relevant metric for this stakeholder
- `[Call-to-Action URL]` - Specific CTA link

### Step 4: Review and Customize
- Adjust tone and formality as needed
- Add organization-specific details
- Remove irrelevant sections
- Add custom metrics or achievements

## 📧 Email Structure Breakdown

### Subject Line Selection
Choose based on stakeholder priority:
- **Technical**: Focus on performance, architecture, integration
- **Business**: Focus on ROI, cost savings, competitive advantage
- **Security**: Focus on threat detection, compliance, risk reduction
- **Community**: Focus on open source, contributions, collaboration

### Content Sections
Each email should include:
1. **Opening Greeting** - Appropriate formality level
2. **Executive Summary** - 2-3 sentences highlighting key value
3. **Key Achievements** - 3-5 bullet points of most relevant achievements
4. **Launch Details** - Version info and key features
5. **Next Steps** - What stakeholders should do next
6. **Call-to-Action** - Specific, measurable actions
7. **Closing** - Professional sign-off with contact information

## 🎯 Stakeholder-Specific Guidance

### Technical Stakeholders
**Focus Areas:**
- Performance metrics (speed, memory, size)
- Architecture improvements (zero dependencies, offline capability)
- Integration capabilities (CI/CD, IDEs, APIs)
- Technical roadmap and future features

**Key Metrics:**
- Scanning speed: 1,000 packages/second
- Memory usage: <50MB
- Detection rate: 99%+
- Dependencies: Zero

**Tone:** Technical, precise, forward-looking

### Business Stakeholders
**Focus Areas:**
- ROI and cost savings
- Risk reduction value
- Competitive advantage
- Implementation timeline and success metrics

**Key Metrics:**
- Annual savings: $135K-$270K
- ROI: 800%-1500%
- Payback period: <1 month
- Risk reduction: 70%+

**Tone:** Professional, results-oriented, strategic

### Security Stakeholders
**Focus Areas:**
- Threat detection capabilities
- Compliance support (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA)
- Enterprise security features
- Audit and reporting capabilities

**Key Metrics:**
- Detection rate: 99%+
- False positive rate: <1%
- Mean time to detect: <5 minutes
- Compliance coverage: 100%

**Tone:** Authoritative, thorough, compliance-focused

### Community Stakeholders
**Focus Areas:**
- Open source contribution opportunities
- Community growth and engagement
- Developer experience and ease of use
- Collaboration and support channels

**Key Metrics:**
- Contributors: 15+
- License: MIT (free)
- Support channels: Discord, GitHub
- Setup time: 5 minutes

**Tone:** Friendly, inclusive, collaborative

## ✅ Best Practices

### DO:
- Personalize with recipient's name and organization
- Focus on most relevant metrics for stakeholder type
- Include clear, specific call-to-action
- Use consistent branding and tone
- Proofread for errors and typos
- Test links and URLs before sending

### DON'T:
- Use generic, one-size-fits-all content
- Overwhelm with too many metrics or details
- Use overly technical jargon for non-technical stakeholders
- Forget to include contact information
- Send without personalization
- Use outdated or incorrect information

## 🔄 Follow-up Process

### After Sending:
1. **Track Engagement**: Monitor email opens and click-through rates
2. **Schedule Follow-ups**: Plan appropriate follow-up timing
3. **Collect Feedback**: Gather stakeholder responses and questions
4. **Update Templates**: Refine based on feedback received

### Meeting Scheduling:
- **Technical**: Schedule technical deep dives or demos
- **Business**: Schedule executive briefings or ROI discussions
- **Security**: Schedule security assessments or compliance reviews
- **Community**: Schedule community calls or contribution meetings
